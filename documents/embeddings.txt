Embeddings and Vector Search

An embedding is a numeric vector representation of a piece of text that captures its
meaning. Texts with a similar meaning get similar vectors, even when they use completely
different words. This is what makes semantic search possible: instead of matching exact
keywords, you compare vectors.

The standard similarity measure is cosine similarity, which is the dot product of two
vectors divided by the product of their lengths. If the vectors are normalised to unit
length beforehand, cosine similarity is simply the dot product. The score is 1.0 for
identical direction and 0.0 for unrelated text.

In a RAG pipeline every document chunk is embedded once at ingestion time and stored. At
query time only the question is embedded, and it is compared against every stored vector.
For a few thousand chunks this brute-force scan in Python is fast enough. For millions of
vectors you would use a dedicated vector index such as FAISS, sqlite-vec or a vector
database, which use approximate nearest neighbour search.

Choosing the right chunk size matters. Chunks that are too large dilute the vector with
unrelated content, while chunks that are too small lose the context needed to answer.
One to three paragraphs, roughly 500 to 1000 characters, is a good starting point.
